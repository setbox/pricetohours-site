How many hours of work does your rent cost?

By Jackson Teixeira. Published October 30, 2026. 3 minute read.

Rent is usually the biggest bill you pay, and it comes back every month. Turning it into hours of work shows how much of your working life goes to keeping a roof over your head, and helps when you compare apartments or think about moving.

How to do it

Divide your monthly rent by your hourly pay. That is the number of hours you work each month just for rent. Divide by your hours per week to see it in weeks.

Rent of $1,500 a month at $25 an hour60 hours a month
At 40 hours a week1.5 weeks of every month
Share of a 173-hour work month34.6%

A work month here is 40 hours × 52 weeks ÷ 12 = 173.3 hours. The rent and wage are example numbers: use your own in the calculator.

The 30% guideline

In the US, the Department of Housing and Urban Development considers households that pay more than 30% of their income on housing to be cost burdened, and more than 50% severely cost burdened. The figure goes back to public housing rent caps set by Congress, and HUD still uses it to measure affordability.

In hours, 30% of a full-time work month is about 52 hours. If your rent costs more than 52 hours of gross pay a month, you are above the guideline. In the example above, $1,500 at $25 an hour is 60 hours, a little over.

Where the guideline falls short

The 30% rule is a rough measure and it is often criticized. Thirty percent of a small income leaves much less to live on than thirty percent of a large one. It also ignores what else the place saves or costs you: a cheaper apartment with a long commute can cost more hours in total once you count the travel time and transport costs.

Count the commute as part of the rent. Add the monthly cost of getting to work to the rent, and add the commuting hours to the hours of work. Two apartments that look far apart in price can end up close in hours.

Using the number

When you look at a new place, compare the change in hours, not only the change in dollars. Moving to an apartment that costs $200 more a month at $25 an hour means 8 more hours of work every month, or 96 hours a year. Ask whether the extra space or the shorter commute is worth those hours.
