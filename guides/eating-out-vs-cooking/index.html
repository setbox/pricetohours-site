Delivery, takeout or cooking: what a meal costs in hours

By Jackson Teixeira. Published October 30, 2026. 4 minute read.

Dinner is one of the few purchases you make almost every day, so small differences in how you buy it grow into big differences over a year. The same plate of food can cost a few minutes of work or well over an hour, depending on whether you cook it, pick it up or have it brought to your door.

One meal, three ways

Take one dinner, a simple pasta dish, and buy it three ways. The prices below are example numbers, not quotes from any restaurant or app, but the shape is typical: delivery adds fees, a tip and often higher menu prices on top of the food.

Cooked at home: ingredients for one serving, $4.0012 minutes of work
Takeout you pick up: $16.00 plus $1.40 tax52 minutes of work
Delivery: $18.00 menu price in the app, $3.99 delivery fee, $2.70 service fee, $4.00 tip, $1.60 tax$30.29
Delivery at $20 an hour1.5 hours of work

At $20 an hour, the delivered meal costs about seven and a half times the home-cooked one in hours of work. The food itself is not the main difference. About 40% of the delivery price is fees, tip and tax.

But cooking takes time too

This is the fair objection, and it matters. Cooking that pasta might take 30 minutes, plus 10 minutes to clean up. That is 40 minutes of your evening. Delivery gives you those 40 minutes back.

So the real trade is this: delivery costs 1.5 hours of work, cooking costs 12 minutes of work plus 40 minutes of your own time. Choosing delivery means paying about 79 extra minutes of work to save 40 minutes at home. On a night when you are exhausted, that can be a good deal. As a habit, it is an expensive way to buy free time.

Takeout you pick up sits in the middle. It skips the delivery fee, the service fee and usually the higher app menu price, and it still saves you the cooking and most of the cleanup.

What a habit costs in a year

One meal is small. The habit is not. Say you order delivery twice a week instead of cooking those two dinners. With the example numbers, each delivered meal costs $26.29 more than cooking. Twice a week for 52 weeks, that is $2,734.16 a year.

At $20 an hour, $2,734.16 is 137 hours of work. That is about 3.4 full work weeks every year, spent on fees, tips and markups for two dinners a week. Switching just one of those two meals to takeout you pick up would cut the yearly gap a lot, without giving up the night off.

Find your own balance

The point is not that delivery is bad. It is a service, and sometimes it is worth every minute. The point is to choose it on purpose, knowing what it costs. A few habits help:

  • Check the total, not the menu price. Look at the final amount on the checkout screen, with fees and tip, before you decide.
  • Pick up when you can. If the restaurant is on your way home, collecting the order yourself removes most of the extra charges.
  • Cook in batches. Making two or three servings at once spreads the 40 minutes of cooking and cleanup across several meals.
  • Keep an easy backup. A few fast meals you can make in 15 minutes stop the tired nights from turning into delivery nights.

Use the number

Next time you open a delivery app, look at the total and divide it by your hourly pay. If the answer is more time than you would spend cooking and cleaning, you are paying to rest, which is fine when you mean it. Try your own yearly gap in the calculator.
