Is overtime worth it? What an extra hour really pays

By Jackson Teixeira. Published October 30, 2026. 4 minute read.

When your manager asks who can stay late, the overtime rate makes it sound like an easy yes. Sometimes it is. But the number on the schedule is not the number that reaches your account, and the extra hours come out of evenings and weekends you cannot get back. Here is how to work out what an overtime hour really pays you.

The basic rule

In the US, the Fair Labor Standards Act says that, unless an employee is exempt, covered employees must receive overtime pay for hours worked over 40 in a workweek, at a rate not less than time and one-half their regular rate of pay. That is how the Department of Labor describes it. Many hourly workers are covered. Many salaried workers in professional, administrative and executive roles are exempt and get no extra pay for longer weeks. Some states add rules of their own, so check your state labor department too.

Time and a half means each overtime hour pays 1.5 times your normal hourly rate. If you earn $22 an hour, an overtime hour pays $33.

What actually reaches you

Overtime is taxed like the rest of your pay. Because it adds to your paycheck, a larger share of it can be withheld than you expect. The example below assumes 25% of the extra pay goes to taxes. Your real share depends on where you live and your situation, so treat it as an illustration.

5 overtime hours at $33 an hour$165.00 gross
After 25% in taxes$123.75
Takeout on two late nightsminus $20.00
What you keep per overtime hour$20.75

The $33 hour is now $20.75. That is still a real gain, but it is less than your normal $22 gross hour once you count the costs that long days bring: a meal you buy because you are too tired to cook, a ride instead of the bus, paying someone to watch the kids. Write down your own costs and subtract them before deciding.

How overtime shrinks a price

There is a good side to this math, and it is worth seeing clearly. Because an overtime hour pays 1.5 times as much, it pays off a purchase 1.5 times faster. A $499.99 game console costs 22.7 hours of work at $22 an hour. At the $33 overtime rate, it costs 15.2 hours.

If you have one specific goal, like a trip, a repair or paying down a card, a few weeks of overtime can get you there much sooner than your regular hours. That is overtime at its best: short, planned and pointed at something you chose.

If you are salaried and exempt

For exempt salaried workers, longer weeks work the other way. Your pay stays the same, so every extra hour lowers what you earn per hour. A $60,000 salary over 40 hours a week for 52 weeks is $28.85 an hour. Work 50 hours a week instead, and the same salary is $23.08 an hour. Every price in your life just got about 25% more expensive in hours.

That does not mean long weeks are never worth it. They can lead to a promotion, a bonus or skills that raise your pay later. But it helps to name the trade honestly, and to know your real hourly rate when you compare prices.

A quick way to decide

  • Know your take-home overtime rate. Gross overtime pay, minus taxes, minus the costs of the long day, divided by the hours.
  • Give the extra money a job. Overtime pointed at a goal feels very different from overtime that disappears into everyday spending.
  • Count what the hours replace. Sleep, family, exercise and rest all have value, even if they do not have a price.
  • Set an end date. A month of extra shifts for a goal is a plan. Every week, forever, is a new normal that is hard to undo.

Use the number

Put your overtime rate into the calculator next to your normal rate and see how much faster a goal arrives. Then check the same price with your after-tax overtime pay, so you know the honest answer.
