About Price to Hours

Price to Hours turns prices into the hours of work it takes to pay for them. It started as a small tool for one question: is this worth the time I traded for it?

It is made by Setbox (SETBOX INFORMATICA LTDA, CNPJ 08.889.601/0001-09), a software studio in Maringá, Brazil. The site is free, needs no account and is supported by ads.

The guides here are general information about personal spending. They are not financial advice. If a number looks wrong, write to us and we will check it.

Who writes it

The calculator and guides are written by Jackson Teixeira, a software developer at Setbox. Jackson builds web products for small businesses and wrote Price to Hours to make one personal finance habit easier: pausing to think about a purchase in hours of work before buying it.

Prices on item pages come from the manufacturer or from news coverage of official price changes, with the source linked and the date it was checked. Minimum wages come from the government agency that sets them.

How we calculate

Price to hours

Hours of work = price ÷ hourly pay. Workdays = hours ÷ hours per workday. Work weeks = hours ÷ (hours per workday × workdays per week).

Salary to hourly pay

A yearly salary is divided by 52 weeks, then by workdays per week and hours per workday. A monthly salary is multiplied by 12 first, because months have different numbers of workdays. A daily rate is divided by hours per workday.

True cost

Free hourly rate = (monthly take-home pay − monthly essential bills) × 12 ÷ 52 ÷ workdays per week ÷ hours per workday. The calculator shows the hours at your pay and at this free rate side by side.

Rounding

Results under one hour are shown in whole minutes. Results under 100 hours are rounded to one decimal place, and larger results to the nearest whole hour. Money is shown to the cent. The same rule is used on every page, so the same price and wage always give the same number.

Limits

All pay figures are before taxes unless a page says otherwise. Taxes, benefits and paid time off vary by person and place, so the results are estimates for thinking about a purchase, not exact accounting.
